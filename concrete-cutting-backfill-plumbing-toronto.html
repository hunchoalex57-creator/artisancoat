The Concrete Part of the Plumbing Job — Handled

When a plumber needs to reach a pipe under your floor, someone has to cut the concrete, dig it out, and then put it all back afterward. Most plumbers don't do that part — so you're left with a broken, open floor and no one to close it. That's the gap we fill. We handle the concrete side from start to finish, so your floor ends up whole, flat, and solid again.

Step 1 · Access

Concrete Cutting & Removal

We saw-cut and break out the exact section of slab your plumber needs to reach — basement floors, garage floors, walkways, and more. Clean cuts, controlled breakout, and full removal of the old concrete, leaving a safe opening ready for the plumbing work.

  • ✓ Precise saw-cutting & controlled breakout
  • ✓ Full removal & disposal of old concrete
  • ✓ Clean, safe opening for your plumber

Step 2 · Dig

Excavation & Digging for Access

Once the concrete is out, we dig out the trench to the depth your plumber needs to expose and work on the pipes. We keep the opening clean and the spoil managed, so the plumbing crew can get in, do their job, and get out.

  • ✓ Trench excavation to the required depth
  • ✓ Clean, managed dig — no mess left behind
  • ✓ Coordinated with your plumber's timeline

Step 3 · Backfill

Backfilling — Done Properly

This is the step that decides whether your floor stays flat or sinks and cracks a year later. After the plumbing is done and inspected, we backfill the trench with the correct compacted gravel base — properly layered and compacted so the ground underneath is stable and ready to hold new concrete.

  • ✓ Correct gravel base, layered & compacted
  • ✓ Stable sub-base so the pour won't settle
  • ✓ The step most crews rush — we don't

Step 4 · Re-Pour

Re-Pouring the Slab — Flat & Level

Finally, we pour the concrete back — matched to the height and finish of your existing floor so the patch sits flush and functions like the rest of the slab. Basement floors, garage floors, walkways, and small pads. We also handle other small concrete pours, like AC, shed, and generator pads.

  • ✓ Poured flush & level with the existing floor
  • ✓ Basement floors, garage floors & walkways
  • ✓ Small pads too — AC, shed & generator

Common Questions

Concrete Cutting & Backfill for Plumbing — FAQ

Straight answers about the concrete side of a plumbing job.

My plumber broke the concrete but won't pour it back — can you do it?

Yes — this is exactly what we do. A lot of plumbers cut and break the concrete to reach a pipe but don't put it back, which leaves you with an open floor. We come in after the plumbing is done, backfill the trench with proper compacted gravel, and re-pour the concrete flat and level so your floor is whole again. Send a photo of the opening to 416-889-8273 on WhatsApp for a fast quote.

Do you cut and remove concrete floors so a plumber can access the pipes?

Yes. We saw-cut and break out the section of slab your plumber needs, remove the concrete, and excavate for access — leaving a clean, safe opening for the plumbing work. Once the plumber is finished, we come back to backfill and re-pour. We can coordinate the timing so the floor isn't open any longer than it needs to be.

Do you backfill the trench after the plumbing is done?

Yes. Proper backfill is the step that makes the repair last. We fill the trench with the right compacted gravel base so the ground underneath is stable — if this is skipped or rushed, the new concrete can sink or crack later. We compact it properly before pouring, so your floor stays flat for good.

Can you re-pour a basement or garage floor after plumbing work?

Yes. We re-pour flat, level concrete floors — basement floors, garage floors, walkways, and small slabs — after plumbing, drainage, or waterproofing work. We match the new pour to the existing floor height and finish so the patch blends in and functions like the rest of the slab.

How fast can you close up an open concrete floor?

As fast as we can schedule it — we know an open floor is disruptive and a trip hazard, so backfill-and-pour jobs get prioritized. Once the plumbing is signed off and the opening is ready, most residential backfill-and-re-pour jobs are completed quickly. Send photos on WhatsApp and we'll give you a timeline with your quote.

Do you work directly with plumbers as a concrete subcontractor?

Absolutely — plumbers are some of our best repeat clients. Most plumbers don't want to break concrete or pour it back, so we handle that part for them: cutting and removing the slab before the plumbing, then backfilling and re-pouring after.
